lock Private Note • My Reading Desk Page 114 • The Garden of Forking Paths
Inscribed 12 Oct 2024

“The labyrinth is not merely geometric; it is temporal and linguistic. Each sentence branched into divergent bifurcations.”

Borges suggests that time itself is an infinite web of choices made simultaneously. Does Ts'ui Pên's novel exist as an impossibility or as a literal depiction of quantum superposition written decades early? The narrator realizes that death and survival are both enacted across different branches.

MA
Marcus Aureliano (Patron #402) Verified Custodian

“The universe (which others call the Library) is composed of an indefinite and perhaps infinite number of hexagonal galleries...”

Notice how the physical geometry of the hexagon dictates the psychological isolation of the librarians. The architecture is totalitarian because it contains every conceivable truth alongside every conceivable falsehood. To search for a specific volume is an act of pious despair.

lock Private Note • My Reading Desk Page 42 • Pierre Menard, Author of the Quixote
Inscribed 18 Sep 2024

The profound irony that writing the identical seventeenth-century text verbatim in the twentieth century yields an infinitely richer, more anachronistic masterpiece. The context of reading creates the book, not the letters. Cervantes’ phrase is mere rhetorical praise of history; Menard’s is an astonishing contemporary interrogation of historical truth.
